Ward 4 · Position 1 · On the Record

Two Records. One Seat.

Kyle served on City Council from 2017 to 2020. Teresa Turk has held this seat since 2019. Here's what each of them brought to Council, and what happened next. Every number comes from the city's own minutes.1

Building Homes vs. Blocking Them

Fayetteville needs more homes. One record adds them. The other subtracts.

Homes on the line

Kyle · Feb. 2020

Saving 100 affordable homes

New Heights Church and Cobblestone Farm, 1210 N. 54th St.

New Heights Church had land on the west side of town, and Fayetteville Housing Partners had a plan to build affordable homes on it. The money depended on a federal housing tax credit tied to that exact site, on a deadline. The land wasn't in the city yet.

Kyle sponsored both the annexation (34.85 acres) and the rezoning, and walked them onto the agenda so the timing would work. The developer told Council the affordable units would be locked in. Both measures passed 7-0 the same night.2

City staff later called it the project "that created 100 new family homes."

Turk · 2020–2026

300 homes appealed. 5 appeals. 0 wins.

Rules that help the city's plan, and rules that fight it

City Plan 2040 calls for more homes and more kinds of homes inside the city, where roads and pipes already exist.5

Kyle · 2019–2020

Housing in the Cultural Arts Corridor. When the city planned new parking for the arts corridor downtown, Kyle's resolution told it to look for ways to add housing too. It passed 7-1. Turk was the only No.8

Walkable neighborhoods. Kyle's street-design ordinance (Ord 6244) points new homes toward the street instead of walling off back yards along busy roads. It passed unanimously.9

Turk · 2024–2025

More expensive homes. Turk's 2025 parking ordinance means every project now needs more parking and has less room for homes. The extra land can break small projects, and the cost gets passed on to homebuyers and renters.6

Fewer homes on College Avenue. When Council rezoned College Avenue (71B) to encourage more housing in the middle of town, Turk proposed new buffers for Urban Corridor lots that would have severely limited the housing the corridor is meant to hold. She tabled it after two readings.7

Fair's fair: Turk sponsored a waiver of about $1,000 in permit fees for New Beginnings' bridge-housing community for people without homes. It passed 7-0.10

Getting Things Done

Bringing an item to Council is easy. Bringing one that changes something in Fayetteville is the job.

Kyle · 14 measures advanced

  • 71% passed and changed policy
  • 14% messaging
  • 7% failed
  • 7% tabled

Turk · 37 measures advanced

  • 54% passed and changed policy
  • 27% messaging
  • 11% failed
  • 8% tabled or withdrawn

A "messaging" resolution changes nothing whether it passes or fails. These are letters to the legislature, positions on another city's policies, and thank-you notes to the school district. Turk had 10 of those. Kyle had 2.11

Of Kyle's 12 measures that passed, 10 were unanimous.12

Half of Turk's failed measures were changes to the Council's own rules and procedures. She wanted to limit how other members attended by Zoom, and to move meetings to 5:00 p.m. even though some of her colleagues have day jobs. Both failed. She also proposed another study of the city's recycling program, and nobody would even second it.13

Where They Agreed

Helping renters through COVID

In September 2020, Kyle asked the City Attorney to draft a ban on rent late fees for the rest of the pandemic. The City Attorney warned that state law likely blocked it, so the ordinance was tabled. Right there in the meeting, Turk suggested a resolution asking landlords to hold off on fees, and Kyle asked to co-sponsor it. Two weeks later their joint resolution, urging landlords to work with tenants and avoid evictions, passed unanimously.14

Picking the Right Fights

Every hour Council spends on a dead-end item is an hour it doesn't spend on yours.

Motions that died with no second

A motion with no second never even gets a vote. Turk made eight. She tried to cut a scooter program in half, rewrite the short-term rental rules mid-debate, reverse an approved housing compromise, and delay a rezoning by three months. Each time, not one colleague backed her.15

Turk's 12 appeals, by what she appealed

GrantedFailed

She went after 300 approved homes and lost every time. But when the Planning Commission said no to a drive-through car wash on Wedington, she appealed to say yes, and won.16

Loosening the rules, then losing anyway

Turk brought four measures to make development appeals easier to file. She cut the number of Council members needed to appeal a permit to two, waived a resident's appeal fee, and let a member from any ward file on a resident's behalf. Three passed.17 Every new appeal means another full public hearing, with staff, the applicant, public comment and debate. Under the looser rules her appeals kept losing: 1-7 on Shiloh, 0-7 on Velma, 1-7 on Ramay.

Kyle · 2019

Kyle's one appeal: fix the street, keep the homes

Neighbors were worried about traffic from the 165-lot Crystal Springs subdivision. Kyle appealed, but he didn't try to stop the homes. His amendment asked for street connections east to Deane Solomon and west to Salem before construction, and it passed unanimously.

The appeal as a whole failed 3-5, and the neighborhood was built.20

Turk · 2025–2026

Taking on the school district, twice

Fayetteville Public Schools needed a site for the new Ramay Junior High. Council debated the rezoning over three meetings in late 2025. Turk moved to delay it (failed 4-4) and voted no. It passed 5-3.18

Five months later, with the zoning settled, she appealed the school's site plan anyway. That meant 25 more public comments and another full hearing. She was the only sponsor and the only Yes vote: 1-7.19 Both sides of that fight run on your tax dollars, with city staff on one side and school district staff on the other.

Claiming Credit

Staff write a lot of good policy. What matters is what a Council member does with it.

Kyle · Wedington trees, 2018

A property on Wedington was cleared of trees using a loophole: a grading permit with no development plan. Staff wrote a fix, but it would take 31 days to go into effect, and the urban forester said two more requests just like it had already come in.

Kyle asked the City Attorney to draft an emergency clause on the spot, then moved to put the ordinance into effect that night to close the loophole immediately. It passed 6-1.25

Turk has described conservation zoning and Pick Me Up as things she brought forward.26 Both are good programs, and she did help with each one. But the record shows who started them.

Turk · Conservation Zoning (CCR)

City planning staff wrote the new conservation zoning district and presented it to Council, which passed it in June 2025. Turk supported it and offered amendments.21 A year later she sponsored an add-on (Ord 6990) banning stormwater ponds on that land. It passed 6-0.22

Turk · Pick Me Up

City staff launched Pick Me Up in 2022 with Genesis Church. It's a litter crew that hires unhoused neighbors.23 At budget time, Turk moved to add $20,000 to keep it going. Council Member D'Andre Jones seconded and said he had planned to make the same motion himself. It passed unanimously.24
